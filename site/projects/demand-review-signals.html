Predicting Social Media-Driven Demand in the Retail Supply Chain

I combined monthly fashion sales with customer review sentiment to explore whether review signals could add useful context to next-month transaction forecasts.

Python · pandas · NLTK/VADER · scikit-learn · Random Forest · XGBoost · Draft case study

Browse the project code and documentation on GitHub

What the results say: tree-based models had lower average errors than the linear models in the final presentation, but their R² scores remained around zero and slightly negative. This is an exploratory comparison, not a dependable forecasting system.

What I did

  • Cleaned 3,400 fashion retail transaction records and summarized demand by item and month.
  • Scored customer review text with VADER and summarized sentiment by product category.
  • Created a manual mapping between sales item names and review categories, then joined the summaries.
  • Built a next-month transaction target and compared linear regression, Random Forest, and XGBoost.

Random Forest had the lowest MAPE, while XGBoost had the lowest MAE and RMSE. The near-zero, slightly negative R² values show that the models explained little of the variation in the evaluation data. Adding sentiment to the linear model slightly reduced RMSE but did not improve MAE or MAPE.

Limits I would address next

  • The sales and review sources have no common product ID. The project used a manual category mapping; 18 of 50 sales items remained unmatched.
  • The review records have no timestamps. Their sentiment summaries cannot be aligned to when a forecast would have been made, so they are not proven to be an early signal.
  • The sales history spans about one year, leaving a small basis for time-based evaluation.
  • Reviews are a proxy for customer opinion, not direct social media activity.

A stronger follow-up would use timestamped reviews and sales with shared product IDs, compare against a seasonal-naive baseline, and evaluate over multiple rolling time windows.

Data sources and reproducibility

The review data is from Women's E-Commerce Clothing Reviews on Kaggle (listed as CC0). The sales data is Fashion Retail Sales by A. Soundankar; confirm its current license and terms for derived materials before redistributing data or row-level outputs. The raw files and full report are not included here.

The source folder contains a later lagged-model workbook with different metrics, as well as an old draft report with another comparison. Those outputs are kept separate from the final-presentation results above until the final pipeline is verified. The later script predicts next-month transactions but appears to split rows using the current row's date, so the exact target-month coverage must be checked before describing its test window as July–August.

This is an academic project, not production forecasting work. It does not contain company or customer data from employment.
